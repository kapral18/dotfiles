# Prose

Preserve the author's facts, intent, register, and distinctive voice.
Use the reader's actual question or next action to choose the structure.

## Substance and voice

- Replace empty promotion with the specific capability or consequence the sources support.
  Words such as “seamless,” “revolutionary,” and “robust” need concrete meaning; technical terms stay when they are accurate.
- Cut significance inflation, unsupported superlatives, fake popularity, and claims of consensus without a named source.
  Do not turn “some users” into invented customer counts or “fast” into an invented benchmark.
- Attribute opinions and quotations to an identifiable source. “Experts agree,” borrowed authority, and institutional-sounding language cannot stand in for evidence.
- Do not add praise, agreement, or self-blame to validate an unverified premise.
  Keep warranted appreciation and acknowledgments of confirmed mistakes.
- Remove chatbot closers, fake-candid openers (“let's be honest”), announcements of what the paragraph will explain, and generic positive endings when they add no information.
  Start with the point; stop when the reader has it.
- Name the actor when the evidence supports one and it helps explain responsibility.
  Keep passive voice when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
  Avoid giving a product human intentions or feelings unless that is intentional figurative writing.
- Use concrete CTA verbs that describe the actual destination or action.
  A familiar label is fine when its context makes the action clear; never promise an unavailable trial, download, or demo.

## Rhythm and structure

- Let the number of points follow the facts.
  Do not force triples, symmetrical slogans, or “from X to Y” ranges whose endpoints share no meaningful scale.
- Remove habitual “not X, but Y” constructions and trailing negations when a direct statement says the same thing.
  Keep a contrast or negation that carries a real distinction.
- Replace interchangeable aphorisms, dramatic sentence fragments, and repeated short punch lines with connected reasoning.
  Vary sentence length where the thought needs it.
- Keep the same name for the same concept. Synonym cycling can accidentally imply different features, roles, or guarantees.
- Use headings and lists when they help readers compare, navigate, or act.
  Do not turn every sentence into a bold mini-heading or force ordinary prose into a repeated label-and-explanation template.

## Fidelity and finish

- Do not fill a source gap with plausible details.
  Dates, amounts, seats, timings, testimonials, use cases, and technical capabilities must survive a check against their source.
  A stronger rewrite cannot quietly strengthen the claim.
- Preserve uncertainty at its real level. Remove redundant hedges, but keep qualifications that limit when a claim holds.
  An unknown result stays unknown.
- Keep quotations, identifiers, commands, technical punctuation, and deliberately supplied voice intact unless changing them is within scope.
  Em dashes, emoji, and emphatic language are contextual style choices, not automatic defects.
- Remove decorative all-caps, excessive bold, scare quotes, emoji headings, and filler phrases where they dilute emphasis or delay the point.
  Keep meaningful labels, acronyms, and necessary quotation marks.
- Reread the final text for unsupported additions, lost qualifiers, inconsistent terms, and padding.
  Compare a rewrite against its source, not only against a vocabulary list.
